2027 presidential election · France, in Saint-Martin

Saint-Martin stands with David Lisnard.

We live and work in Saint-Martin, from Marigot to Grand-Case, from Quartier-d'Orléans to Sandy Ground. We support David Lisnard because his priorities meet those of the island's families: the cost of living, work for our young people, a roof over our heads since Irma, safety in our neighbourhoods.

Why David Lisnard

Liberty, Order, Dignity

Nouvelle Énergie's three founding words, summarised from the official programme page (in French).

Liberty

Less tax and regulatory control by the State: free choice of school, funded pensions, self-government for local authorities.

Order

“In the streets as in the public accounts”: a State refocused on education, security, justice, diplomacy and health.

Dignity

Respect for every French citizen as an individual, made possible by liberty and order.

Saint-Martin first

Eight proposals for the island

The national programme has no chapter on the overseas territories. So we started from what families here live with: the price of water, unemployment, housing, safety. These proposals come from our local group: each starts from a sourced fact, builds on a measure in the programme and says who decides. Proposals from our group, not commitments from the candidate

1Local freedom

A right to adapt national rules to the island

The Territorial Council could ask for a national rule to be adapted, and get a quick answer from the State.

Who decides · The State, through a revision of the organic law on the island's status. 2Water

Drinking water, a top priority

Drinking water costs more than twice the national average, and nearly a third is lost in the network.

Who decides · The Collectivité and its water authority, with support from the State. 3Jobs

Make Saint-Martin a priority hiring zone

Apply here first the negative employer contributions planned for hiring people on the RSA (minimum income).

Who decides · The State (social contributions). How it fits with the Collectivité's own taxes remains to be studied. 4Housing

Owning your own home

Fewer than one household in three owns its home, against nearly two in three nationally.

Who decides · The Collectivité (planning, housing) and the State (savings, grants). 5Security

Step up OFAST's work on the island

The national anti-drug office already has a unit in Saint-Martin: the programme's “dedicated task forces” should reinforce it.

Who decides · The State, with the Netherlands for cooperation. 6Border

Firm on immigration, with discernment

Priority to removing foreign nationals convicted of crimes and to enforcing the conditions for access to benefits.

Who decides · The State. 7Health

A hospital run closer to home

Without the regional health agency (ARS), the Marigot hospital would be run closer to the island.

Who decides · The State (by law), then the Collectivité and the prefect. 8Schools

Two pilot schools for autonomy

The two new secondary schools opening in 2026 could try out the autonomy set out in the programme.

Who decides · The State (national education), with the Collectivité as owner of the buildings.

A France that stands firm

Law and order and the big national issues

Measures taken from the programme's topic pages, translated from French.

Security

  • Give prefects real command, with local room to manoeuvre on staff and equipment.
  • Put security forces back on the ground: at least 50% of their time, against 37% today.
  • Allow willing municipalities to arm their municipal police.In Saint-Martin, which has no municipalities, this role falls to the Collectivité's territorial police. Source
Security page of the programme (in French) →

Immigration

  • End automatic citizenship by birth on French soil.
  • Systematically expel foreign nationals convicted of serious offences and foreign nationals on the security watch list (fiche S).
  • Make non-contributory social benefits conditional on five years of legal residence.
Immigration page of the programme (in French) →

Health

  • Abolish the regional health agencies (ARS) and transfer their responsibilities to prefects and elected officials.
  • Give mayors back the chairmanship of hospital boards.In Saint-Martin, which has no mayor, this means the President of the Collectivité, who already chairs the supervisory board of the Louis-Constant-Fleming hospital (2023). Source
  • Better reimbursement of care in under-served areas.
Health page of the programme (in French) →

Schools

  • Abolish school catchment areas and gradually introduce an education voucher.
  • Give head teachers the freedom to recruit, choose their methods and manage their budget.
  • Create an end-of-primary-school certificate required to enter secondary school.
Schools page of the programme (in French) →
